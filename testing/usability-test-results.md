StudySync – Usability Test Results

1. Overview

StudySync is a mobile adaptive study-planning application designed to help university students manage courses, academic tasks, deadlines, study sessions, reminders, and academic progress.

Usability testing was performed on the Balsamiq mobile prototype to evaluate its ease of use, navigation, clarity, and usefulness.

The evaluation focused on five representative university-student users and five core tasks covering the main StudySync user journey.

The purpose of the evaluation was to identify usability problems before finalizing the prototype and to determine which areas required design revision.

3. Test Tasks

Participants were asked to complete five representative tasks.

Task 1 – Login and Dashboard

Log into StudySync and locate the main Dashboard.

Task 2 – Create a Task

Create a Database Systems assignment with a due date, priority, study time, and reminder.

Task 3 – Use the Adaptive Recommendation

Find the StudySync recommendation for the assignment and add the recommended study session to the Study Planner.

Task 4 – Check Progress

Find the student's weekly progress and determine the number of completed tasks.

Task 5 – Change Study Preferences

Open Settings and change the preferred study time.

5. Task 1 – Login and Dashboard

Result

All five participants successfully completed the login and Dashboard task without assistance.

Success rate: 5/5

Observations

Participants were generally able to identify the Login action and understand that the Dashboard was the main starting point after authentication.

The Dashboard's primary navigation and major functions were relatively easy to identify.

Interpretation

The login flow and primary Dashboard navigation were considered usable and required no major redesign.

---

6. Task 2 – Create an Academic Task

Result

Four participants completed the task independently.

One participant required additional clarification when entering task information.

Independent completion: 4/5

Completion with difficulty: 1/5

Observations

The Add Task screen provided the expected information fields, including:

- Task title
- Course
- Due date
- Priority
- Estimated study time
- Reminder
- Notes

The main difficulty was understanding how some task attributes related to the resulting study schedule.

Interpretation

The task-creation process was generally understandable but could benefit from clearer labels and more explicit feedback after saving a task.

---

7. Task 3 – Adaptive Study Recommendation

Result

Three participants completed the task independently.

Two participants required clarification about the purpose of the recommendation and how it was generated.

Independent completion: 3/5

Completion with difficulty: 2/5

Observations

The adaptive recommendation was one of the less immediately understandable features.

Participants understood that StudySync was recommending a study session, but some were unsure why a particular time had been selected.

The recommendation needed a stronger explanation connecting:

deadline → workload → available time → recommended study session

Interpretation

The adaptive feature is valuable but requires stronger explanation and feedback.

This was identified as a priority area for revision.

---

8. Task 4 – Check Weekly Progress

Result

Four participants completed the task independently.

One participant required clarification when interpreting the progress percentage.

Independent completion: 4/5

Completion with difficulty: 1/5

Observations

The Progress screen clearly communicated that academic performance was being tracked.

However, a percentage such as "90%" did not immediately communicate how many tasks had actually been completed.

The Weekly Statistics screen helped provide additional detail.

Interpretation

Progress information should combine percentages with concrete values such as:

«28 of 31 tasks completed.»

This allows users to interpret their progress more quickly.

---

9. Task 5 – Change Study Preferences

Result

Four participants completed the task independently.

One participant experienced minor difficulty locating the Settings option.

Independent completion: 4/5

Completion with difficulty: 1/5

Observations

Once Settings was located, the preference controls were understandable.

The main issue was discoverability of Settings from the Profile area.

Interpretation

Settings should remain accessible through a clearly identifiable Profile navigation path.

---

10. Overall Results

Across the five tasks, all participants were ultimately able to complete the required activities.

There were:

- 25 total task attempts
- 20 independent completions
- 5 completions with difficulty or clarification
- 0 unsuccessful completions

This represents an overall independent completion rate of:

80%

The remaining 20% of attempts were completed after participants experienced minor difficulty or required clarification.

---

11. Positive Findings

Several aspects of the prototype performed well.

11.1 Clear primary navigation

Participants were generally able to understand the main mobile navigation structure.

11.2 Task management was understandable

The Add Task and Task Details screens provided the information expected for academic task management.

11.3 Progress tracking was useful

Participants understood the purpose of the Progress section and could identify their academic performance.

11.4 Reminder functionality was understandable

Reminder controls communicated the idea of receiving notifications for upcoming academic activities.

11.5 Adaptive planning was considered useful

Although the adaptive recommendation required clearer explanation, the concept was understandable and useful once explained.

---

12. Main Usability Problems

The most important usability problems identified were:

1. The adaptive recommendation did not clearly explain why a particular study session was suggested.
2. Some task fields could be clearer for first-time users.
3. Progress percentages could be difficult to interpret without supporting numbers.
4. Settings was not immediately discoverable for every participant.
5. Some interactions needed stronger feedback after an action was completed.

---

13. Usability Priorities

Priority| Problem| Reason
High| Adaptive recommendation explanation| Core differentiating feature of StudySync
Medium| Progress interpretation| Important for monitoring academic performance
Medium| Task form clarity| Core task-management function
Medium| Settings discoverability| Affects personalization
Low| Confirmation feedback| Improves confidence after actions

---

14. Conclusion

The usability evaluation indicates that the StudySync mobile prototype provides a generally understandable experience for the main academic-planning tasks.

All participants were ultimately able to complete the five core tasks. However, several areas required improvement, particularly the explanation of adaptive recommendations, interpretation of progress information, task-form clarity, and Settings discoverability.

These findings were used to guide revisions to the Balsamiq prototype.

The resulting revisions are documented in "findings-and-revisions.md".
